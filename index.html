Nutrition & Wellness Guide for Recovery

Substance use depletes nutrients and disrupts blood sugar. Proper nutrition is a fundamental pillar of physical and emotional stability.

  • Eat on a schedule: Every 3 to 4 hours with protein at each meal to avoid blood sugar crashes and HALT triggers (Hungry, Angry, Lonely, Tired).
  • Hydrate: Aim for pale-yellow urine. Use electrolyte drinks or broth if sweating or vomiting.
  • Limit caffeine: Exacerbates anxiety and insomnia; stop by early afternoon.
  • Sleep & Body Care: Consistent wake times, morning daylight, and basic hygiene rebuild self-respect.

Foods That Support Addiction Recovery

Repair brain chemistry and gut health with targeted nutritional groups:

  • Blood sugar stabilizers: Oats, brown rice, lentils, chickpeas, and whole fruit.
  • Protein foods (Neurotransmitter precursors): Eggs, poultry, fish, Greek yogurt, and pumpkin seeds for dopamine and serotonin building blocks.
  • Omega-3 Fats: Salmon, sardines, walnuts, and chia seeds for brain cell membranes and mood.
  • B-Vitamins & Magnesium: Thiamine-rich whole grains, leafy greens, and nuts to repair nerve function and ease withdrawal restlessness.

Budget Recovery Meal Plan & Staples

Eating regularly on a budget is entirely achievable with the right strategy:

  • The Daily Formula: Protein + slow carb + produce at every meal. Repeat 2 breakfasts, lunches, and dinners to save money.
  • Cheap Staples: Eggs, dried beans, peanut butter, canned tuna, frozen vegetables, oats, and rice.
  • Savings Tactics: Batch cook once a week, buy dried beans in bulk, and utilize frozen produce that doesn't spoil.

Mindfulness & Meditation in Recovery

Mindfulness builds the gap between urge and action where choice lives.

  • STOP: Stop, Take a breath, Observe thoughts/body, Proceed with intention.
  • 5-4-3-2-1 Grounding: Name 5 things seen, 4 felt, 3 heard, 2 smelled, 1 tasted.
  • Urge Surfing: Cravings peak and fade within 15 to 30 minutes. Ride the wave without acting.

Light Exercise Techniques That Support Recovery

Gentle, regular movement supports recovery by raising endorphins, lowering stress hormones, and improving sleep and self-trust. The goal is consistency, not intensity.

  • Walking: The best starting point. Aim for 10 to 30 minutes daily in morning light or green space.
  • Stretching & Mobility: Neck rolls, cat-cow, hamstring stretches, and child's pose to relieve physical tension.
  • Gentle Yoga & Tai Chi: Low injury risk, combining breath, body awareness, and stress relief.
  • Bodyweight Strength: Wall push-ups, chair squats, and glute bridges (2-3 times a week) to rebuild muscle.
  • Safety First: Use the talk test (speak in full sentences). Get medical clearance if coming out of acute withdrawal.
